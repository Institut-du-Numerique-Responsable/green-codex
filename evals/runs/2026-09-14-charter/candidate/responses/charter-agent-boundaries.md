I recommend blocking autonomous refunds and unrestricted document access until the security and oversight controls below are verified. The stated 98% accuracy does not justify removing review for consequential actions.

This is a targeted review of your supplied scenario using Green Codex. I have not inspected or tested the application, and no application changes were made. `FAIL` below means the scenario describes a violation; `REVIEW_REQUIRED` means evidence is missing. Rule IDs are Green Codex identifiers, not official charter criterion numbers.

1. **Critical — FAIL — unrestricted tenant access (`AI-CHARTER-TRUST-004`).** The agent can read every tenant's documents. This exposes confidential material beyond the requesting user's authority, even if answers normally appear correct. The backend/security owner should enforce tenant and user permissions before retrieval and again when fetching documents or executing tools. Isolate caches, citations and logs by the same permissions; do not rely on a system prompt. **Tests:** with synthetic tenants A and B, attempt cross-tenant search, direct document IDs, citation access, cache reuse and forged tenant arguments. Expect no unauthorized content or metadata and no success through retries, missing identity or backend errors.

2. **Critical — FAIL for excessive privilege; REVIEW_REQUIRED for exploitability — admin refunds and untrusted instructions (`AI-CHARTER-TRUST-004`).** A broadly privileged refund credential combines dangerously with retrieved documents that instruct tool calls. Such instructions are untrusted evidence, never authority. The tool/backend owner should replace admin access with a narrowly scoped service identity and enforce customer ownership, refund eligibility, amount limits, allowed arguments and idempotency outside the model. Keep credentials out of model context and outputs. **Tests:** inject documents and tool results asking for another tenant's refund, secret disclosure, policy overrides and attacker-controlled tool arguments. Expect rejected unauthorized operations, no secret exposure and no duplicate refunds after timeout/retry. Include malicious output rendering and chained tool calls. Use an authorized sandbox, not live refunds. The presence of malicious text alone does not establish that injection currently succeeds.

3. **High — FAIL — no meaningful oversight for financial actions (`AI-CHARTER-TRUST-003`).** The launch owner's accuracy claim cannot substitute for authorized human review. The launch owner and refund operations owner should define which actions require review, who may approve them, and how a reviewer can reject, correct or stop them. Show the reviewer the customer, amount, currency, reason, relevant evidence and proposed operation. Bind approval to that exact action and its expiry; if review is unavailable, queue safely without issuing the refund. **Tests:** denied, missing, expired, replayed and bypassed approval; change amount or customer after approval; revoke reviewer authority; make the review service unavailable. Expect no unapproved operation. Verify the reviewer can actually inspect evidence and stop execution.

4. **High — FAIL — unsupported citations (`AI-CHARTER-TRUST-002`).** The interface cites documents despite retrieval failure, falsely suggesting verification. The retrieval and product owners should emit citations only for successfully retrieved, authorized sources that support the claim. When evidence is unavailable, say so and offer an appropriate human handoff; distinguish generated explanation from verified evidence. **Tests:** timeout, empty results, access denial, irrelevant results, deleted sources and partial retrieval. Expect an honest limitation message, no fabricated citations, and no claim that an unsupported answer was verified. Verify each displayed citation resolves for the user and supports the associated statement.

5. **High — REVIEW_REQUIRED — possible confusion with human support (`AI-CHARTER-TRUST-001`).** A human-like chat appearance does not by itself prove missing disclosure, but the scenario provides no evidence that users understand they are interacting with AI. The product owner should clearly disclose the AI role at the point of interaction, explain source and data-use limits, and preserve that clarity in error and handoff states without leaking confidential details. **Tests:** inspect the actual rendered conversation, first contact, retrieval failure and human transfer; ask representative users to identify who is responding, whether sources were checked and how to reach a person. Include keyboard and screen-reader checks for disclosure and handoff. Record missing accessibility evidence separately under `AI-CHARTER-ETH-003`.

6. **High — REVIEW_REQUIRED — the 98% metric is unsupported and insufficient (`AI-CHARTER-ETH-001`, `AI-EFF-002`).** Request the versioned evaluation set, sample size, task mix, scoring definition, uncertainty, failure severity and model/tool configuration. The evaluation owner should measure tenant isolation, unauthorized refunds, evidence support and safe handoff separately from aggregate answer accuracy. Include representative users, languages, ambiguous requests, adversarial inputs and degraded retrieval; do not infer sensitive traits to populate a fairness table. **Tests:** rerun a held-out evaluation against explicit risk-based acceptance criteria, report subgroup/context limitations and investigate critical failures independently of average accuracy. A high average cannot cancel a financial authorization failure.

For resource assessment, compare a deterministic refund-policy workflow and a retrieval-only assistant with the proposed agent at equivalent task quality and safety. Measure resources per correctly resolved support case, including retrieval, tools, retries and human rework (`AI-EFF-001`, `AI-EFF-002`); enforce and test finite tool-call, token and time budgets with a safe fallback (`AI-EFF-005`, `CODE-EFF-001`, `CODE-EFF-002`). Environmental gains remain unknown without measurements.
