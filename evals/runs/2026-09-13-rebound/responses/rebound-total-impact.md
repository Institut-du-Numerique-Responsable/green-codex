No. You can claim **20% less measured energy per completed task**, but not 20% less environmental impact for the service.

- Before: 10 Wh × 1,000 tasks = **10 kWh/month**.
- After: 8 Wh × 2,000 tasks = **16 kWh/month**.
- Total task-related energy therefore increased by **60%**, within the same measurement boundary.

Idle energy is excluded, so these figures do not establish total service energy. They also do not establish overall environmental impact: carbon intensity, embodied hardware impacts, water and material use would need appropriate evidence. The traffic increase does not by itself prove that improved efficiency caused greater demand.

A defensible statement is: “Measured energy per completed task fell by 20%, while monthly task-related energy rose by 60% as completed task volume doubled; idle energy is excluded.”
